The Rocket Flight Database is distributed as two comma-separated-value (CSV)
files together with a plain-text reproducibility statement. The first file,
\texttt{flight\_comparison.csv}, is the headline corpus: 25 instrumented
flights with paired apogee predictions from a commercial trajectory simulator
(RASAero~II \cite{rogers2015,rogers_rasaero_alt}) and from the open-source
predictor described in the companion research article \cite{yu2026jsr}. The
second file, \texttt{sounding\_rockets\_exploratory.csv}, is a separate
exploratory set of historical high-Mach sounding-rocket flights that is
deliberately held outside the headline corpus. Throughout both files, all
apogee values are reported in feet (ft) and all error values in percent~(\%);
peak Mach is dimensionless and body diameter is in inches~(in). No values in
the two distributed files are rounded beyond the precision shown; the
single-decimal \texttt{err\_*\_pct} columns are convenience columns recomputed
downstream from the full-precision apogee columns
(see \texttt{reproducibility.md}).

\subsection{File 1: \texttt{flight\_comparison.csv} (25-flight headline corpus)}

Each row is one flight. The 14 columns are defined in
Table~\ref{tab:cols_comparison}. The corpus spans peak Mach 0.54 to 4.33 and
body diameters from 1.75~in to 8.00~in; two flights are two-stage vehicles for
which a slash-separated pair of stage diameters and motor designations is
recorded (AeroPac~104K Two-Stage and MESOS~293K).

The \texttt{flight\_data\_type} column records how the measured apogee was
obtained, taking one of four values in this file:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Barometric Altimeter} --- apogee from an onboard pressure
        altimeter (17 of 25 flights);
  \item \textbf{GPS} --- apogee from an onboard GPS receiver (3 flights:
        A-601~Kinsel, AeroPac~104K Two-Stage, MESOS~293K);
  \item \textbf{Optical Track} --- apogee from ground-based optical tracking
        (3 flights: Kline-Rogers~L500 and the two Full~Metal~Jacket flights);
  \item \textbf{Integrated Accelerometer} --- apogee from twice-integrated
        onboard accelerometer data (2 flights: Proteus~6 and Qu8k).
\end{itemize}
The measured-truth, RASAero~II, and ``this work'' columns are produced
independently of one another, so the per-flight comparison is non-circular;
the provenance split is documented in \texttt{reproducibility.md}.

All 25 flights have a measured apogee, a RASAero~II apogee, and a this-work
apogee. The corpus contains no flight above Mach 4.33; the highest-Mach record
is the two-stage MESOS~293K closure (peak Mach 4.33, measured apogee
293{,}488~ft), for which this work records an apogee of 273{,}056~ft
($-6.96\%$). The value-of-the-data and detailed comparison statistics are
presented in the companion research article \cite{yu2026jsr}; this article
describes the data only.

\subsection{File 2: \texttt{sounding\_rockets\_exploratory.csv} (exploratory
high-Mach set)}

The second file holds 20 historical high-Mach sounding-rocket flights
simulated with the same open-source predictor and reported in full as an
exploratory capability set that is excluded from the headline corpus. Each row
is one flight; the eight columns are defined in
Table~\ref{tab:cols_exploratory}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Column dictionary for \texttt{sounding\_rockets\_exploratory.csv}.}

\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}lll X@{}}
\toprule
Column & Type & Unit & Description \\
\midrule
\texttt{vehicle}             & string & ---  & Vehicle / flight designation. \\
\texttt{source\_doc}         & string & ---  & Primary published source for the measured apogee (radar / beacon / GPS), cited per row. \\
\texttt{real\_apogee\_ft}    & integer & ft  & Measured (ground-truth) apogee. \\
\texttt{orp\_apogee\_ft}     & integer & ft  & Apogee predicted by the open-source predictor of this work (blank for transparently reported simulation-error rows). \\
\texttt{err\_thiswork\_pct}  & float  & \%   & Signed relative apogee error of this work vs.\ measured (blank where no apogee was produced). \\
\texttt{peak\_mach}          & float  & ---  & Peak flight Mach number (blank where no trajectory was produced). \\
\texttt{headline\_admissible} & string & --- & Whether the flight falls within $\pm 10\%$; values are ``within +/-10\%'', ``NO (>10\% error)'', or ``NO (sim error)''. \\

Of the 20 exploratory flights, 3 fall within $\pm 10\%$ of the measured apogee:
Black~Brant~V~VB (AAF-VB-32) at peak Mach 7.224 with error $-6.97\%$
\cite{dtic_ad0733141}, Nike-Deacon no.~1 at peak Mach 4.956 with error
$-1.06\%$, and Nike-Deacon no.~2 at peak Mach 5.079 with error $-0.89\%$. The
remaining 17 flights fall outside $\pm 10\%$ and are reported transparently:
the Nike-Apache family over-predicts ($+24$ to $+36\%$), the Nike-Cajun
University~of~Michigan sounding flight over-predicts ($+16.56\%$), and the
Arcas (blunt and secant-ogive) and HEROS~3 flights under-predict ($-29$ to
$-69\%$). This count of 17 also includes two transparently reported
non-nominal solver outcomes: one zero-apogee record (Nike-Apache NASA~14.210~GI,
\texttt{orp\_apogee\_ft}~$=0$, \texttt{err\_thiswork\_pct}~$=-100.0$,
flagged ``ZERO APOGEE'' in the note) and one simulation-error record
(Nike-Cajun Hurricane, with \texttt{orp\_apogee\_ft},
\texttt{err\_thiswork\_pct} and \texttt{peak\_mach} left blank and marked
``NO (sim error)''). As with File~1, all apogees are in feet and all errors in
percent. The full set is published here so that the exploratory outcomes are
reported without selection.
