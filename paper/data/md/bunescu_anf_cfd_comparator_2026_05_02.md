# Published-CFD Comparator: Bunescu et al. 2025 URANS vs ORP on the Basic Finner

**Date:** 2026-05-02
**Status:** B-level CFD comparison; substantial residual gap matches the
documented ADA636861 underprediction.

## Source paper

Bunescu, I.; Hothazie, M.-V.; Stoican, M.-G.; Pricop, M.-V.; Onel, A.-I.;
Afilipoae, T.-P. "Numerical Study of the Basic Finner Model in Rolling Motion."
*Aerospace* **2025**, *12*, 371. MDPI, open-access (CC BY 4.0).
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/aerospace12050371

Citation taken from the title page of the PDF in repo
(`paper/data/pdf/aerospace-12-00371-v2.pdf`). Authors are affiliated with the
National Institute for Aerospace Research "Elie Carafoli" (INCAS), Bucharest,
and the University Politehnica of Bucharest. The article is an extended version
of AIAA SciTech 2024 Paper 2024-2348.

## CFD method (verified from PDF Section 2.3)

- Unsteady Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (URANS)
- Realizable k-epsilon turbulence model (sliding-mesh for rolling cases)
- Mesh: ~9.6e6 cells, prismatic boundary-layer mesh, y+ > 30
- Domain: 100 m spherical far-field around 0.6 m model
- Validated by the authors against the existing Basic Finner experimental
  database (referenced in their introduction)

## Geometry confirmation (Figure 2 of the paper)

The Bunescu CFD model is the **Army-Navy Basic Finner / ANF**:

- 60 mm diameter, 600 mm length (L/D = 10)
- 10-deg half-angle conical nose, 2.84 calibers long
- Cylindrical afterbody, 7.16 calibers long
- Four rectangular fins: 1.0 cal chord, 1.0 cal exposed span, 0.04 cal thickness
- Sharp leading and trailing edges
- Reference area S = pi*d^2/4

This matches the OpenRocket Plus benchmark geometry
(`SupersonicTestRockets.makeBasicFinner()`) caliber-for-caliber. The OpenRocket
benchmark uses a 30 mm aeroballistic-range model (per ADA636861), but
non-dimensional coefficients are directly comparable. The only meaningful
difference is Reynolds number (Bunescu's 60 mm full-scale CFD is at higher
Re_d than the 30 mm aeroballistic model).

## Interpretation

This is **not a clean validation** -- ORP underpredicts Bunescu's URANS by
24-59 % across the swept range. The result is honest publication evidence,
not a regression gate. Three points anchor the interpretation:

1. **The gap is consistent with the existing ADA636861 ANF benchmark.**
   The ORP Basic Finner total-drag benchmark
   (`BasicFinnerDragBenchmarkTest`) already documents a MAPE of ~22.7 % vs
   the ADA636861 free-flight aeroballistic data, with the same direction
   (ORP underpredicts) and the same Mach pattern (worst near the
   transonic peak, recovering at high Mach). Bunescu's URANS sits **above**
   the ADA636861 free-flight values at the matching Mach, so the ORP-vs-CFD
   gap is wider than the ORP-vs-experiment gap. This is the expected
   ordering: free-flight aeroballistic data (sting-free, finite Re) is the
   ground truth, CFD on a 60 mm full-scale model overpredicts at the
   transonic peak, and ORP is the most aggressive underprediction. None of
   these three lines coincide exactly.

2. **Reynolds-number mismatch is part of the story.** The ORP benchmark
   geometry is the 30 mm aeroballistic-range model; Bunescu's CFD is the
   60 mm full-scale wind-tunnel calibration model. Re_d differs by roughly
   a factor of two at matching Mach, which is not enough to explain the
   full gap but is part of it.

3. **The single C_N point at AoA = 10 deg, M = 1.6 is the worst miss
   (-63 %).** Bunescu reports C_N = 3.4; ORP gives 1.25. ORP's normal-force
   slope in the ANF supersonic regime is documented to be conservative
   (NASA TM X-653 anchoring puts CNa MAPE at 8 % across M = 0.6-5.82) but
   that benchmark uses the NASA NSCFB blunt-fin geometry, not the ANF.
   The ANF-specific C_N gap at high AoA may indicate that the
   Pitts-Nielsen-Kaattari interference factor or the crossflow C_d for
   the cylinder-fin combination is biased low for this exact geometry.

## Reviewer-defensible language for the paper

> "B-level CFD comparison: OpenRocket Plus was compared against the
> open-access URANS k-epsilon CFD of Bunescu et al. (2025) on the
> Army-Navy Basic Finner over Mach 0.4-3.5 at zero AoA. ORP
> systematically underpredicts the CFD axial-force coefficient by
> 24-59 % (MAPE 39 %), with the largest gap in the low-transonic
> regime and convergence at high supersonic. The gap is consistent
> in sign and magnitude with the existing ADA636861 free-flight
> benchmark (MAPE 23 %), and is bounded by the same Reynolds-number
> mismatch and base-drag-on-finned-flat-base modeling limitations
> documented for that case. We treat this CFD comparator as
> publication evidence rather than a regression gate, and we do not
> tune model constants to close the gap absent a second independent
> CFD anchor on matching geometry."

## Honest caveats

- Hand-digitization read uncertainty: +/- 0.03 in C_X (about +/- 5 % of the
  transonic peak), +/- 0.2 in C_N at AoA = 10 deg. This does not change the
  sign or the order of magnitude of the gap.
- Bunescu values are roll-averaged at AoA = 0 (where the model is symmetric,
  so the roll average is trivial); at AoA = 10 deg the roll averaging
  smooths out fin-shock interaction patterns that would otherwise show up
  as structured oscillations in C_N vs phi.
- The C_N point at AoA = 10 deg has the largest digitization uncertainty
  because Figure 10e is plotted on a 0-25 vertical scale with closely
  spaced AoA = 0/10/20 curves at M = 1.6.

## Secondary CFD anchor (Sahu, Nietubicz, Steger 1983) -- not exercised

A secondary CFD source -- Sahu, Nietubicz, Steger 1983, Thin-Layer
Navier-Stokes on a secant-ogive-cylinder-boattail at M = 0.9-1.2 -- is
also in repo at
`paper/data/pdf/Empirical heuristics and tuned constants validation/NUMERICAL COMPUTATION OF BASE FLOW FOR A Projectile at Transonic Speed.pdf`.
Citation verified from the title page: ARBRL-TR-02495, Sahu/Nietubicz/Steger,
US Army Ballistic Research Laboratory, June 1983, AD-A130-293. Figure 14
plots base drag CFD circles (M = 0.9-1.2) overlaid with experiment and McDrag.

This was **not** exercised as a comparator in this iteration because the
Sahu geometry is a finless secant-ogive-cylinder-**boattail** projectile
that is structurally different from the Basic Finner and would require
building a separate ORP rocket model to compare against. The existing
boattail+base-drag pipeline in ORP (Viswanath boattail correction,
Chapman-Korst free shear layer, Devan-Ashwood turbulent base) is the
right code path for that comparison, but standing up the geometry,
digitizing Figure 14, and writing the test is a separate work item. It
is the obvious next step if a reviewer asks for a second CFD anchor on
a different geometry class.
