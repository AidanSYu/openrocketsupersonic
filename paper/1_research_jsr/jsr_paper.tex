\begin{abstract}
OpenRocket, the most widely used open-source rocketry flight simulator, is
restricted to subsonic aerodynamics. We extend its semi-empirical Barrowman
framework to compressible flight through a once-per-timestep \emph{shock-geometry
pre-pass} that resolves local post-shock flow conditions along the airframe and
exposes them to every downstream drag, normal-force, and stability calculator.
The pre-pass unifies classical compressible methods---Taylor--Maccoll cone flow,
Prandtl--Meyer expansion, oblique and normal shock relations, Van~Driest~II
skin friction, the DATCOM fin wave-drag method, and Modified Newtonian impact
pressure---within a single open implementation. Twenty-one component models are
verified against wind-tunnel and computational references (NACA Report~1135,
RM~A52H28, TN~3393, AGARD-B, and published URANS and Navier--Stokes solutions).
The integrated simulator is validated against a 25-flight ground-truth corpus
spanning Mach~0.54 to~4.33---24 single-stage amateur high-power flights and one
two-stage closure (MESOS~293K). The mean signed apogee error is $-0.38\%$
(95\% bootstrap confidence interval $[-2.41, +1.72]$) with root-mean-square
error $5.34\%$, and all 25~flights fall within $\pm10\%$ of measured apogee. On
a 15-flight prospective holdout the model is \emph{more} accurate (mean absolute
error $4.20\%$) than on the 10 development flights ($5.56\%$), indicating that
the two corpus-frozen base-drag constants generalize rather than overfit.
Against RASAero~II on the 25 paired flights there is no statistically significant
difference in absolute error (Wilcoxon $p = 0.58$). High-Mach sounding-rocket
prediction is reported separately as exploratory: of 20 historical flights only
three fall within $\pm10\%$, with systematic motor- and geometry-reconstruction
errors elsewhere. All source code, the flight database, and the analysis scripts
are released for end-to-end reproduction.
\end{abstract}
