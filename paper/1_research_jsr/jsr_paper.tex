\begin{abstract}
OpenRocket, the most widely used open-source rocketry flight simulator, is
restricted to subsonic aerodynamics. We extend its semi-empirical Barrowman
framework to compressible flight through a once-per-timestep \emph{shock-geometry
pre-pass} that resolves local post-shock flow conditions along the airframe and
exposes them to every downstream drag, normal-force, and stability calculator.
The pre-pass unifies classical compressible methods---Taylor--Maccoll cone flow,
Prandtl--Meyer expansion, oblique and normal shock relations, Van~Driest~II
skin friction, the DATCOM fin wave-drag method, and Modified Newtonian impact
pressure---within a single open implementation. Twenty component models are
externally benchmarked against wind-tunnel, ballistic-range, free-flight, and
computational references (NACA Report~1135, RM~A52H28, TN~3393, NASA TM~X-653,
the DTIC~AD0487365 range data, and published URANS and Navier--Stokes solutions).
The integrated simulator is validated against a 25-flight ground-truth corpus
spanning Mach~0.54 to~4.33---23 single-stage amateur high-power flights and two
two-stage flights (AeroPac~104K and the MESOS~293K closure). The mean signed
apogee error is $-0.38\%$ (95\% bootstrap confidence interval $[-2.41, +1.72]$)
with root-mean-square error $5.34\%$, and all 25~flights fall within $\pm10\%$ of
measured apogee. On a decontaminated 12-flight blind holdout the model is
\emph{more} accurate (mean absolute error $3.95\%$) than on the 13 development
flights ($5.47\%$), indicating that the two corpus-frozen base-drag constants
generalize rather than overfit. Against RASAero~II on the 25 paired flights there
is no statistically significant difference in absolute error (Wilcoxon $p = 0.615$).
High-Mach sounding-rocket
prediction is reported separately as exploratory: of 20 historical flights only
three fall within $\pm10\%$, with systematic motor- and geometry-reconstruction
errors elsewhere. All source code, the flight database, and the analysis scripts
are released for end-to-end reproduction.
\end{abstract}
